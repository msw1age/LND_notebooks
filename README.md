# LND_notebooks
The jupyter notebooks I have made to analyze the neuropixel and widefield data I am working with, and some of the videos I have made.

Currently I have added notebooks for data visualization, PCA, and most things I have done with DMD and its variants. I'll outline what is in the DMD folder:

1) Exact DMD Overview: Here I explain the derivation of DMD and try fitting DMD to the widefield temporal components. I introduce some of the first analysis steps one does with DMD methods (looking at modes, eigenvalues, frequencies, and reconstruction). I introduce relative squared error as a metric and explain its interpretation. We see that DMD fails at reconstructing our data for more than a few seconds.

2) OptDMD Overview: I explain what OptDMD is and how it differs from exact DMD. I then use OptDMD to determine the dimensionality of the widefield data and compare this to the estimate from PCA. I show that, unlike exact DMD, OptDMD is actually capable of qualitatively reproducing the widefield data. Next I apply the formula from David Donoho's optimal SVD hard threshold to the data's singular values and to the optdmd fit's amplitudes and find that I obtain the same result. I show how it is possible to reconstruct mouse brain videos showing the dynamics of the reconstruction and of the individual modes.

3) MrOptDMD Overview: I explain my version of MrOptDMD and its connection to gradient boosting. I show that MrOptDMD is able to mostly reproduce the low frequency range of the data with high temporal accuracy, and achieve a much lower RSE than OptDMD on a large window. Next I explore various methods to reduce the complexity of the model. I then show the contributions from each level of MrOptDMD and the eigenvalue spectrum. Next we see that MrDMD completely fails with the same parameters as MrOptDMD. I then show that MrOptDMD is able to decompose an entire experiment into a hierarchical mode structure (this involves a .pkl file that is too large for GitHub; if you would like it please just ask). Finally I create a synthetic dataset and show that MrOptDMD is able to extract the correct modes in the correct places without any prior knowledge beyond a decent estimate for a range of values for the correct number of modes at each level.

4) HankelDMD Overview: I explain the idea behind HankelDMD. I show how it is possible to overfit the data with the d parameter and how one can tell, and then show a fit with a good d parameter. I show via the eigenvalue spectrum that HankelDMD finds eigenvalues that are much more stable than OptDMD or MrOptDMD but with much smaller (in the absolute value sense) negative real parts than exact DMD. I then show the modes found by HankelDMD. Next I show that this stability + much slower decay allows us to forecast with HankelDMD with decent accuracy when combined with timeshifting (i.e. the forecast predicts the major events that will happen in the mouse brain in the next ~5 seconds, but there is a small uncertainty (<1 second) regarding when exactly they will occur). I then create a video showing the true data vs the time shifted forecast.

5) DMD On Stacked Widefield+Neuropixel Data: I explain the setup of fitting DMD to widefield temporal components stacked on top of smoothed neuropixel data and my normalization methods. I justify timeshifting the widefield data and then compute the optimal timeshift. I compare fitting widefield data stacked on neuropixel data from the same time versus widefield data stacked on neuropixel data on 30 different time windows and show that the aligned fit performs significantly better than the misaligned fit when the number of modes is less than 26. I also show that the aligned fit performs the same as the widefield only fit when the number of modes is greater than 20.

6) Time stability of OptDMD Modes: I explain the setup of learning modes from the training data and fitting the weights on future data. I show that this method recovers the true amplitudes when the training data and future data are the same. Next I show that the performance with this method is significantly better when the future data comes immediately after the training data rather than far in the future. I then explore a different method where instead of fitting one set of weights to the future data, I fit a new set of weights either every time instant or every few time instants. We see with this that many of the modes appear to transiently turn "on" and off in the forecast. Next I use this method with a constraint on the minimum amplitude to compute another estimate for the true rank of the data, and see that the result obtain is very close to the optimal SVD hard threshold. Finally I show that this method works well with OptDMD modes but not at all with PCA modes.
